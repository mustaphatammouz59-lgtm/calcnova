How to Pick the Right Calculator

Start from the problem in front of you. Everyday arithmetic — the basic calculator above handles it; step up to the scientific calculator for powers, roots, and trigonometry, and the percentage calculator for discounts, tips, and percent change. Fractions and whole numbers — the fraction calculator adds, simplifies, and converts; the least common multiple and greatest common factor calculators find common ground between numbers, and the factor calculator breaks a number down completely. Algebra — solve equations with the quadratic formula calculator, and work powers and roots with the exponent, log, and root calculators. Statistics — summarize a data set with statistics or mean, median, mode, range, measure spread with standard deviation, and go further with the z-score, confidence interval, and probability calculators. Geometry — the triangle calculator solves any triangle from partial information, backed by the Pythagorean theorem and right triangle calculators, while area, volume, and surface area cover shapes in two and three dimensions. Other bases and big numbers — convert and compute with the binary and hex calculators, and use scientific notation and the big number calculator when ordinary calculators run out of digits.

A note on accuracy

Math leaves less room for approximation than most subjects, so these tools are built to be exact where exactness exists: factors, greatest common factors, and least common multiples are computed with integer arithmetic and are never rounded. Decimal results are computed in double precision and shown to 12 significant digits — enough to expose a repeating decimal for what it is instead of hiding it. Statistics and geometry use the standard textbook formulas (sample vs. population standard deviation is always labeled, so you know which one you are getting), and every calculator is checked against reference examples before it is released.

FAQ

Are these calculators free?

Yes — no sign-up, and everything runs in your browser. Nothing you type is sent to a server.

What can the basic calculator on this page do?

Everyday arithmetic with a running expression display, memory keys (M+, M-, MR), an Ans key for the last answer, EXP for entering scientific notation, and RND for a random decimal. For powers, roots, trigonometry, and logarithms, open the Scientific Calculator.

How accurate are the results?

The integer tools — factors, greatest common factor, least common multiple — are exact. Decimal results are computed in double precision and displayed to 12 significant digits, and the statistics and geometry tools use the standard textbook formulas, each checked against reference examples before release.
