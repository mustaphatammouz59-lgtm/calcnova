An estimate, not a diagnosis

Estimated GFR is a screening number built from creatinine, which muscle mass and diet can shift. A single low value is a reason to talk to a clinician and re-test, not a verdict — kidney disease staging uses GFR together with evidence of kidney damage over time.

What is glomerular filtration rate (GFR)?

GFR describes how fast fluid is filtered through the kidneys, and it is the standard yardstick of kidney function: higher is generally better, and a significant fall means the kidneys are filtering less volume per unit time than they should. Adjusted for body surface area, the normal range is 100 to 130 mL/min/1.73 m2 in men and 90 to 120 mL/min/1.73 m2 in women under 40; after 40, GFR declines progressively with age.

Chronic kidney disease stages

A GFR above 60 mL/min/1.73 m2 is usually adequate, and on its own does not mean chronic kidney disease (CKD).1 A significant decline since a previous test, though, can be an early indicator — and the earlier kidney disease is caught and treated, the better the chance of avoiding dialysis. Stages combine the GFR range with evidence of kidney damage, as noted:

Measuring GFR

The most accurate measurement is inulin clearance: inulin is freely filtered and is neither metabolized, secreted, nor reabsorbed, which makes it ideal — but the test needs continuous intravenous infusion with repeated, carefully timed blood and urine collections, so it is impractical for routine care.2,3 Everyday practice therefore estimates GFR from serum creatinine. Creatinine clearance has real limits: muscle mass moves serum creatinine independently of kidney function, and because the body secretes a little creatinine, clearance based on it typically overestimates GFR by 10-20%. It remains in use because it is easy. Three widely used estimating equations, plus the children's formula, are:

The IDMS-traceable MDRD study equation

GFR = 175 × (SCr)-1.154 × (age)-0.203 × (0.742 if female) × (1.212 if Black), with SCr in mg/dL.

The CKD-EPI formula

GFR = A × (SCr/κ)α × 0.993age, where κ is 0.7 for females and 0.9 for males; α is -0.329 (female) or -0.411 (male) when SCr ≤ κ and -1.209 when SCr > κ; and A is 144 (non-Black female), 166 (Black female), 141 (non-Black male), or 163 (Black male).

The Mayo Quadratic formula

If SCr < 0.8 mg/dL, use 0.8 mg/dL. GFR = e(1.911 + 5.249/SCr − 2.114/SCr2 − 0.00686 × age − (0.205 if female)).

Estimated GFR for children — Schwartz formula

GFR = 0.413 × height (in cm) / SCr.
