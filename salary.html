How pay conversion works

Every conversion on this page runs through one anchor: the annual salary. An hourly wage annualizes by multiplying wage × hours per week × 52 — $25 × 40 × 52 = $52,000. From the annual figure, everything else is division: ÷ 52 for weekly, ÷ 26 for bi-weekly, ÷ 24 for semi-monthly, ÷ 12 for monthly, ÷ 4 for quarterly, and back down to an hourly rate by dividing by the year's scheduled hours (hours per week × 52 = 2,080 at full time). Going the other way — starting from a monthly salary or an annual offer — the same chain runs in reverse, which is what makes the calculator useful for comparing a job quoted one way against a job quoted another.

Unadjusted vs. adjusted: the two columns

The unadjusted column pretends the year has no time off in it: at 5 days a week you work all 260 weekdays. The adjusted column subtracts the holidays and vacation days you entered. Ten federal-style holidays plus fifteen vacation days removes 25 days — 200 hours — leaving 235 working days (1,880 hours), a factor of about 0.904. What that factor does depends on how you are paid. For an hourly worker whose days off are unpaid, the adjusted column is what actually lands in the bank: $25 an hour becomes $47,000 a year, not $52,000. For a salaried worker paid through vacations, the annual figure never changes — instead the adjusted column reveals the effective rate for the days truly worked: that same $52,000 salary is really $27.66 for each hour worked, not $25.00. The calculator follows one convention so the two columns stay comparable: hourly and daily inputs are read as unadjusted rates, while weekly, monthly and annual inputs are read as adjusted (real-world) amounts and grossed up into the unadjusted column.

Pay frequencies, untangled

  • Weekly — 52 paychecks a year. Common for hourly and part-time work.
  • Bi-weekly — every two weeks, 26 paychecks. Two months a year contain a third paycheck, which is why bi-weekly × 26, not × 24, gives the annual figure.
  • Semi-monthly — twice a month on set dates (the 1st and 15th, say), exactly 24 paychecks. Each check is bigger than a bi-weekly check on the same salary: $2,166.67 vs. $2,000 at $52,000.
  • Monthly — 12 paychecks; typical for salaried professionals in many countries.
  • Daily — the rate for one scheduled day: hourly wage × hours per day (hours per week ÷ days per week).

Confusing bi-weekly with semi-monthly is the most common salary-math mistake: pricing a $2,000 bi-weekly offer as if it were semi-monthly overstates the salary by $4,000 a year.

Salary, wages, and the numbers around them

A wage pays for time worked — an hourly rate times hours, with overtime (under the U.S. Fair Labor Standards Act, typically 1.5× the regular rate past 40 hours a week for non-exempt workers) and unpaid time off both moving the total. A salary is a fixed annual amount divided across the pay schedule, largely insulated from weekly hours. The federal minimum wage of $7.25 an hour annualizes to $15,080 at full time (many states set higher floors), while the median full-time worker earns well above that — which is why offers are negotiated in annual terms but lived in weekly ones. Two more conversions worth keeping straight: gross vs. net — everything here is gross; federal and state taxes, FICA, retirement contributions and premiums come out before the deposit, and the Income Tax Calculator estimates what remains — and paid time off as pay: 25 days of PTO on a $52,000 salary is worth about $5,000 a year, a real difference when comparing an offer with generous leave against a slightly higher wage with none.

Frequently asked questions

How do I convert an hourly wage to an annual salary?

Wage × hours per week × 52. $25 × 40 × 52 = $52,000. Divide an annual salary by 52, then by hours per week, to go back to hourly.

What is the difference between bi-weekly and semi-monthly pay?

Bi-weekly: every two weeks, 26 checks a year. Semi-monthly: twice a month on fixed dates, 24 checks. Same salary, different slice sizes — $2,000 vs. $2,166.67 at $52,000 a year.

What does "adjusted" mean here?

Adjusted figures remove your holidays and vacation days from the working year. They show what an hourly job actually pays if the time off is unpaid — or, for salaried staff, the effective rate per hour truly worked.

How many work hours are in a year?

2,080 scheduled hours at 40 × 52. With 10 holidays and 15 vacation days: 1,880 hours actually worked.
