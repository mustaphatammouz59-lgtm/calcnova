APR vs Interest Rate: Why the Bigger Number Tells the Truth

The interest rate prices the principal and nothing else. The APR prices the loan: interest plus the lender fees, discount points and mortgage insurance you must pay to get it, annualized over the term. U.S. law (the Truth in Lending Act) forces lenders to disclose it precisely so offers can be compared side by side. Worked example, from the defaults above: a $100,000 loan advertised at 6% with $2,500 of upfront fees carries a real APR of 6.563% — the fees quietly added more than half a point. On the mortgage side, a 6.2% loan with $3,500 of fees and half a point comes out at 6.367%. When two lenders quote the same rate, the APR is where the difference hides.

What a mortgage APR includes — and what it leaves out

Typically inside the APR: origination and processing fees, underwriting, discount and origination points, mortgage broker fees, mortgage insurance, and some closing charges. Typically outside it: appraisal, survey and title fees, notary and recording charges, and the prepaid escrows for taxes and homeowner’s insurance (real costs, but not charges for credit). Because the boundary varies a little by lender, ask each lender for the itemized fee list behind their APR before deciding — the Loan Estimate you receive within three days of applying has exactly that breakdown.

The fine print: APR assumes you stay the course

APR spreads upfront fees across the entire term — 30 years of payments diluting a one-time charge. Pay the loan off early, as most borrowers do by selling or refinancing, and those same fees are in effect spread over far fewer years, so the effective cost is higher than the stated APR. The practical rule: if you expect to keep the loan a long time, chase the lowest APR; if you expect to move within a few years, weight low upfront fees more heavily than a tenth of a point of APR. Points make the same trade in miniature — each point (1% of the loan) buys a lower rate, and only pays for itself if you hold the loan past its own break-even, which our refinance calculator explores for existing loans.

Fixed, variable — and APR vs APY

A fixed APR stays put for the life of the loan; a variable APR rides an index (plus a margin based on your credit), so its disclosed figure is only the starting point. Do not confuse APR with APY either: APY is the deposit-side measure and includes compounding, which is why the same money looks bigger as APY. Ten percent APR compounded monthly equals 10.47% APY — banks advertise loans by APR (looks smaller) and savings by APY (looks bigger), and now you know why. FAQ

What is the difference between APR and interest rate?

The rate prices the principal only. APR annualizes interest plus fees, points and mortgage insurance — the total cost of credit — so it is higher and far better for comparing offers.

Why is my mortgage APR higher than the quoted rate?

Closing costs. Origination fees, points and insurance are spread over the term and added to the rate. A low rate with heavy fees can produce a higher APR than a higher rate with light fees.

Is the lowest APR always best?

Only if you keep the loan to term. APR assumes you do; early payoff makes upfront fees matter more than the APR implies.
