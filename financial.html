How to Pick the Right Calculator

Start from the decision, not the math. Buying a home? The chain runs house affordability → down payment → mortgage, with the real estate hub collecting every housing tool — including FHA, VA, refinance, and rent-vs-buy — in one place. Buying a car? Price the loan on the auto loan calculator, settle a rebate-versus-rate offer with cash back or low interest, or weigh leasing on the auto lease calculator. Carrying debt? Check the ratio lenders see on the debt-to-income calculator and the payoff path on the loan calculator. Growing money? The compound interest calculator is the workhorse while the investment suite is being built.

A note on accuracy — and on SOON

Every calculator here is built from the underlying formulas — amortization, annuity math, IRS and HUD rules where they apply — and checked against published reference examples before release; where a reference's own shortcut differs from the governing rule (it happens), the page follows the rule and says so. Results are planning estimates: a lender's quote can differ by rounding, fees, and the day's rates. As for the SOON entries: this page doubles as CalcNova's public roadmap. The mortgage, real-estate, and auto sections are complete; investment and retirement tools are next, in search-demand order. If a calculator you need is marked SOON, it is coming — the list above is the order book.

FAQ

Are these calculators free?

Yes — no sign-up, and everything runs in your browser. Nothing you type is sent to a server.

How accurate are they?

Verified against published reference results to the cent before release. Treat them as precise planning estimates — final quotes still depend on your lender, fees, and current rates.

Why are some entries not links?

SOON means planned, not built. We label them instead of linking to pages that don't exist — no dead ends.
