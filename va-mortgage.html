How VA Loans Work

A VA loan is a mortgage from a private lender, partly guaranteed by the U.S. Department of Veterans Affairs. That guarantee is what unlocks the terms no other mainstream loan matches: no down payment required, no mortgage insurance at any down payment, and no prepayment penalty. Eligibility runs through your service record — most borrowers confirm it with a Certificate of Eligibility (COE) — and the loan must be for a primary residence. The single mandatory cost unique to VA loans is the funding fee, modeled exactly above.

The VA funding fee (official chart)

The funding fee is a one-time charge, expressed as a percentage of the loan amount, that keeps the program running for the next veteran. For purchase and construction loans it steps down as your down payment grows — and jumps for repeat use at low down payments:

Loan typeDown paymentFirst useSubsequent use
Purchase / ConstructionLess than 5%2.15%3.30%
Purchase / Construction5% to 10%1.50%1.50%
Purchase / Construction10% or more1.25%1.25%
Cash-Out Refinance—2.15%3.30%
IRRRL (streamline refinance)—0.50%0.50%
Loan Assumption—0.50%0.50%
Manufactured Home (not permanently affixed)—1.00%1.00%

The current chart is uniform across eligibility categories — active duty, Guard and Reserve, and surviving spouses pay the same purchase rates; category decides entitlement, not the fee. Two levers do move the fee: your down payment tier, and paying it in cash instead of financing it (financing is allowed, but you then pay interest on the fee for the life of the loan — the results above price that difference). And for many borrowers the fee is simply zero: veterans receiving compensation for a service-connected disability, and surviving spouses of veterans who died in service or from a service-connected disability, are exempt.

No PMI — the quiet superpower

Put 3.5% down on an FHA loan and you pay mortgage insurance for the life of the loan; put under 20% down on a conventional loan and PMI follows you until 80% LTV. A VA loan charges neither — ever. On the default loan above, the one-time funding fee replaces roughly $32,500 of conventional PMI. That is why, for anyone with entitlement, VA is usually the cheapest low-down-payment path even after the fee, and why comparing loans on rate alone misleads: compare total insurance-type costs, as the box above does. Two more structural pluses: there is no VA loan limit for borrowers with full entitlement (lenders set their own ceilings), and sellers may contribute up to 4% of the price toward your closing costs. On the qualifying side, lenders lean on a 41% back-end debt-to-income guideline plus a residual-income test rather than a hard cutoff — check your fit with the house affordability calculator.

Prepayments

Federal regulation (38 CFR §36.4314) guarantees the right to prepay a VA loan at any time, in whole or in part, with no premium or fee. The extra-payment fields above are therefore pure upside: every extra dollar goes to principal, shortens the term, and cuts total interest — with the largest effect in the early years, when the balance (and the financed funding fee inside it) is highest.

FAQ

How much is the VA funding fee?

2.15% of the loan at under 5% down on first use (3.30% on subsequent use), dropping to 1.50% at 5–10% down and 1.25% at 10%+. IRRRL and assumption loans pay a flat 0.50%.

Who is exempt from the funding fee?

Veterans receiving compensation for a service-connected disability, and surviving spouses of veterans who died in service or from a service-connected disability, pay no funding fee at all.

Should I finance the funding fee or pay it in cash?

Financing preserves your cash but you pay interest on the fee for decades — on a $10,750 fee at 7.3%, roughly $15,800 of extra interest over 30 years. If the cash is available without draining your reserves, paying upfront is usually cheaper.

Can I use my VA loan benefit again?

Yes — entitlement is reusable. Just note the subsequent-use funding fee (3.30%) when putting down less than 5%.
